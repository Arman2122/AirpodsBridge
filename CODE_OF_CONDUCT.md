# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Argue about code, not about each other.

## What is expected

This project is small, and most interaction is one person explaining a problem to another.
That works when everyone:

- Assumes the other person is acting in good faith, especially across a language barrier.
  Contributors here are not all native English speakers.
- Keeps criticism on the work — the design, the diff, the bug report — and not on the
  person who produced it.
- Accepts that "no" is a legitimate answer. The project's scope is deliberately narrow, and
  a declined feature is not a personal judgement.
- Gives a reason when saying no, rather than closing something in silence.
- Respects that maintainers do this in their spare time. Nobody is owed a review on a
  schedule.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks.
- Discrimination or demeaning comments based on who someone is — including age, body,
  disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality, appearance, race,
  religion, or sexual identity and orientation.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission. Note that
  trace logs can contain Bluetooth device names and addresses; redact before posting.
- Sustained disruption: repeatedly reopening settled decisions, or filing issues to make a
  point rather than to solve a problem.

## Scope

This applies everywhere the project happens — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, and code review — and to anyone representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to the maintainer, [@Arman2122](https://github.com/Arman2122), by direct
message on GitHub. Reports are handled privately, and the reporter's identity is not
disclosed.

Depending on severity and history, the response may be a private warning, having a comment
edited or removed, a temporary block, or a permanent ban from the repository. Serious or
repeated behaviour goes straight to the more severe end.

If the person you need to report is the maintainer, GitHub's
[report abuse](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
process goes over their head.
